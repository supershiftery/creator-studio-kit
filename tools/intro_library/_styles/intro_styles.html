Why approved intros still fall short

I measured every intro you've judged. Pace and smoothness are already close to the best one. The real difference is how many different kinds of source get pulled in.

The one you called perfect (the reselling spreadsheet) used 4 kinds of source and only 22% of its shots were your own product. The "decent" ones mostly lean on 2 kinds and 70-100% product footage. That's exactly where you said "too much of the watch" and "too much of the phone".

New rule for every intro: 4+ kinds of source, product footage at most half, and an outside asset about every 2 lines. The self-check now warns when an intro falls under that.

    What gets approved

    • Every shot shows the literal thing he says (product, brand, site, person, place)
    • His own footage first, then real pages, real photos, then stock that acts out the word
    • Full-frame footage; cards and paper only as a 1-2 page accent
    • A new real source every ~1.5-2 s, never the same clip twice
    • Shots land ON the word (dissolve starts early), smooth sub-pixel glides at 60 fps
    • Problem → turn → payoff; Minecraft drops on the turn and ends on a title slam
    • Story sounds tied to his words, quiet and varied
    • Ends 0.3 s after the last word, straight into "hey guys"

    What gets rejected

    • Cartoon FX (burst, fire, smoke, swirl), emojis, memes, ransom-note or comic text
    • Flashes and light leaks in phone intros, colour-panel wipes, risers, 808s
    • Black, white or empty frames; narrow intros with bars; small cards on a dark wall
    • Holds over 3.5 s, repeats, 4+ web pages, too much of the product
    • Wrong-context pictures, random stock people, the muted talking cam, his link page
    • Numbers he didn't say, readable codes, dates, highlighted filler words
    • Too busy: pops on every word, random zooms, shaky camera, jumpy clips
