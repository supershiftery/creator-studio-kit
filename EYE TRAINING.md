# Eye Training

You train your eye by **noticing and naming**. Claude does the heavy lifting (cutting, rendering, checking). Your job
is to watch, say what feels off, and say *why*. Every note you give gets turned into a rule, so the studio gets better
with every video, and so do you.

## The loop (every video)

1. **Record** as usual. Don't worry about mistakes: say the line again and keep going. The cutter keeps your last take.
2. **Record the intro voiceover** separately (15–35 s): problem → "but in today's video…" → the reveal.
3. **Drop it into the studio** and let it build a half-size draft.
4. **Watch the draft once without stopping**, like a viewer would. Write down only the moments where you *felt* something:
   bored, confused, a jump, too loud, "huh?".
5. **Watch again and write timed notes** in the studio (Review + notes), using plain words:
   `1:22 clipped word` · `2:03-2:10 keep this` · `3:40 music too loud` · `5:10 show the screen here` · `intro too slow`.
6. **Ask Claude "why did that happen?"** for each note. Have it explain the rule and change the rule, not just the spot.
7. **Approve → final → upload.** Then tell Claude what made this one good. It becomes your reference video.

## Weekly drills (20–30 min each)

| Drill | How | What you learn |
|---|---|---|
| **Seam listening** | Ask Claude for 20 random cut points from your last draft as 4-second clips. Rate each: invisible / noticeable / bad. | where cuts hide (on breaths, between sentences) |
| **Level meter** | Ask Claude for the same 30 s at three music levels (-28 / -32 / -36 LUFS). Pick one blind. | what "under the voice" means |
| **Intro teardown** | Pick a top video in your niche. Ask Claude to fetch the first 30 s as a contact sheet and transcript, then list every visual change, every sound, and when each lands. | literal scenes, pacing, why the hook works |
| **Outlier study** | Run the competitor tracker. Take the top 3 outliers this week: write down the title, thumbnail, first line and video length. Ask Claude what they share. | what your niche clicks on |
| **Compare to final** | When you edit one yourself, give Claude both files. It aligns them and lists what you kept that it cut, and the reverse. | your own taste, measured |
| **Rule review** | Ask Claude: "Show me every rule that changed this week and the note that caused it." | your style, written down |

## Questions to ask yourself while watching

- Did I know what the video was about in the first 5 seconds?
- Is there a moment with nothing new on screen for more than ~4 seconds?
- When I say "this", "here" or "as you can see", am I *seeing* it?
- Is the music noticeable? (If you notice it, it's too loud.)
- Did any cut make me blink?
- Would I keep watching if this were someone else's video?

## How to give a good note

Bad: "the middle is weird"
Good: "4:10–4:40 drags: I'm explaining while the screen sits still. Speed up the scrolling, or zoom on what I'm reading."

A good note says **where**, **what you felt**, and (if you know) **what would fix it**. Claude handles the how.

## Milestones

- **Week 1–2:** you can spot clipped words and loud music on the first watch.
- **Week 3–4:** your notes drop from ~15 per draft to ~5. You start predicting what Claude will get wrong.
- **Month 2:** you approve drafts in one round, and your notes are mostly about creative choices (intro ideas, story order) instead of mistakes.
- **Month 3:** you have 3–5 reference videos and a playbook in your own words.
